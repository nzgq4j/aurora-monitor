A camera collects light for seconds at a time, so it picks up colour and faint aurora your eyes can't. A few settings make the difference.

## With a camera

- **A tripod** or anything steady. Exposures last several seconds.
- **A wide lens** opened as far as it goes: f/2.8 or wider if you have it.
- **Manual focus at infinity.** Set it in daylight and mark the lens, or focus on a bright star.
- **ISO 1600** to start; go up to 3200 or more for faint aurora, down for bright.
- **Exposure 5 to 15 seconds.** Shorter when the aurora is moving fast, so the curtains stay sharp; longer when it's faint and still.
- **White balance** around 3500 K, or auto. Shoot RAW if you can, so you can adjust later.
- Turn off the flash and lens stabilisation on the tripod.

Take a test shot, look at it, and adjust: brighter or darker with the exposure time or ISO.

## With a phone

- Use **night mode** and the main lens, not the wide or zoom lens.
- Hold the phone against something solid, or use a small tripod. Any movement blurs the shot.
- Tap to focus on the sky and lower the brightness slider a little if the photo comes out washed out.
- Turn on **Night mode** in Polar Lights while you shoot, so the screen doesn't spoil your night vision. See [Night mode and language](/support/start/night-mode-and-language).

## Tips

- **Give your eyes 20 minutes** in the dark, away from street lights. Looking at a white screen undoes it.
- **Include a foreground**, a tree, a mountain or a building, for a sense of scale.
- **Cold drains batteries.** Keep spares in an inside pocket.
- **Post it.** Share your best shots in the [gallery](/support/start/post-to-the-gallery).
