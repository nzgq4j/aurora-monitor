## Terms

- **Active region:** an area of strong magnetic field on the Sun, usually with sunspots, where flares and CMEs start.
- **Aurora borealis / australis:** the northern and southern lights.
- **Auroral oval:** the ring around each magnetic pole where aurora is most frequent.
- **Bz:** the north–south part of the solar wind's magnetic field. Negative (south) lets energy into Earth's field.
- **CME (coronal mass ejection):** a cloud of plasma and magnetic field thrown out from the Sun.
- **Coronagraph:** a telescope that blocks the Sun's disc to show the faint corona and CMEs.
- **Coronal hole:** a dark region in ultraviolet images where fast solar wind escapes.
- **Flare:** a sudden burst of light and X-rays from an active region, classed C, M or X, each ten times stronger than the last.
- **G scale:** NOAA's geomagnetic storm scale, G1 (Kp 5) to G5 (Kp 9).
- **Halo CME:** a CME seen spreading all round the Sun, heading towards Earth or directly away.
- **Hemispheric power:** the energy the aurora puts into one hemisphere's upper atmosphere, in gigawatts.
- **IMF:** the interplanetary magnetic field, the Sun's field carried by the solar wind.
- **Kp:** a 0 to 9 index of how disturbed Earth's magnetic field is over 3 hours.
- **L1:** the point about 1.5 million km towards the Sun where solar wind monitors sit.
- **Magnetic latitude:** latitude measured from Earth's magnetic poles, which decides how often you see aurora.
- **Magnetosphere:** the region of space controlled by Earth's magnetic field.
- **nT (nanotesla):** the unit for the solar wind's magnetic field.
- **OVATION:** NOAA's model of where aurora is likely in the next 30 to 90 minutes.
- **S scale:** NOAA's solar radiation storm scale, from high-energy protons.
- **Shock:** a sudden jump in solar wind speed and density, often the front of a CME.
- **Solar wind:** the stream of charged gas flowing out from the Sun.
- **Substorm:** a sudden release of stored energy that makes the aurora brighten and move.
- **Sunspot number:** the standard measure of solar activity.
