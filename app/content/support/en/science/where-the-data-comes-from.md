Polar Lights doesn't make its own measurements. It brings together live data from space agencies and research centres and works out what it means for your place.

## The solar wind: DSCOVR and ACE

NOAA's **DSCOVR** and NASA's **ACE** spacecraft sit at the L1 point, about **1.5 million km** from Earth towards the Sun, measuring the solar wind before it reaches us. Depending on the wind's speed, it takes about **15 to 60 minutes** to travel on from L1 to Earth, so that's the warning they give. NOAA's **SOLAR-1** spacecraft (launched as SWFO-L1), at the same point since January 2026, also carries the CCOR-2 coronagraph.

## The aurora: NOAA's OVATION model

The **OVATION** model turns the solar wind measured at L1 into a map of the chance of aurora overhead around each pole, for roughly the next 30 to 90 minutes. It updates every few minutes. It drives the **Auroral oval** map, the map's Aurora layer, the **Aurora** number in **Your sky** and the first hours of the **Tonight** score.

## Kp

The **Kp** index comes from GFZ Potsdam's network of ground magnetometers and NOAA's estimates, with NOAA's forecast for the next three days and its 27-day outlook further ahead.

## The Sun

- **NASA's SDO** (Solar Dynamics Observatory) takes the ultraviolet images, the visible-light picture of sunspots and the magnetogram.
- **ESA and NASA's SOHO** carries the LASCO coronagraphs that show CMEs leaving the Sun.
- **NOAA's GOES-19** satellite carries the SUVI ultraviolet telescope, the CCOR-1 coronagraph and the X-ray sensors that measure flares and the proton sensors behind the radiation scale.
- **NASA's DONKI** database gives the WSA-Enlil model's forecasts of CME arrival.

## Weather and darkness

- **Cloud forecasts** come from Open-Meteo; the **satellite cloud check** from EUMETSAT's infrared images.
- **Darkness and the Moon** are calculated in the app from your position and the time.
- **City lights** on the map come from NASA's Black Marble.
